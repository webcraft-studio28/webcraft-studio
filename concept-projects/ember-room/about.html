Our Story

About Ember Room

A small kitchen built around one idea: everything tastes better cooked slowly, over real fire.

Ember Room started as a question: what if the whole menu, start to finish, only ever touched fire? No gas burners, no shortcuts. Just wood, embers, and a lot of patience.

The kitchen is built around a single wood-fired hearth, visible from most of the dining room. Nothing about it is hidden. You can watch dinner get made the same way it's been made for a very long time, just done well.

The menu changes with what the season actually gives us, not a printed list that stays the same all year. Some weeks that means more citrus, other weeks it means whatever the farm had too much of.

Est. 2021 One hearth, one seating, one menu that keeps changing

How We Cook

One Seating

Design Notes

Why this concept looks the way it does

The problem

Most restaurant sites either bury the menu behind a PDF download or lean entirely on food photography, which is expensive to shoot well and dates quickly as the menu changes.

The approach

A dark, moody, multi-page site (not a single scroll) built around typography and a live-fire visual motif instead of photography, so the menu itself reads as clean HTML that's easy to keep current.

The result

A distinct visual identity, elegant serif type paired with a warm ember palette, that reads as upscale without needing a single stock photo.
