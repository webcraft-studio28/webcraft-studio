5 SEO Mistakes Small Business Websites Make

Most small business websites don't have a dramatic SEO problem — they have a handful of small, fixable ones that quietly add up. Here are the five I see most often, in roughly the order I'd fix them.

1. Every page has the same title tag

Open your browser tab on a few different pages of your own site. If they all say the same thing — usually just your business name — you're wasting one of the highest-impact pieces of on-page SEO you have. Each page's title tag should describe what that specific page is about, ideally with the words a customer would actually search for. "Plumbing Services in Austin | Your Business Name" tells Google (and searchers) far more than "Home."

2. No mobile optimization

Google indexes and ranks your site primarily based on how it performs on mobile, not desktop — this has been the default since 2019. If your site requires pinch-zooming, has buttons too small to tap, or breaks its layout on a phone screen, it's actively working against you in search results, regardless of how good it looks on your laptop.

3. Slow load times

Page speed is both a ranking factor and a conversion factor — a slow site loses visitors before they even see your content, and Google notices that too. Large uncompressed images are the most common cause on small business sites. A single unoptimized photo can add several seconds to load time.

4. Thin or missing meta descriptions

The meta description doesn't directly affect ranking, but it's the preview text people see in search results before they click. A missing or generic one means you're leaving your click-through rate up to chance. Two sentences that clearly state what you offer and why someone should choose you is enough.

5. No clear heading structure

A page should have one H1 (the main heading), followed by a logical hierarchy of H2s and H3s underneath. Skipping straight to bold, oversized paragraph text instead of real headings makes it harder for both search engines and screen readers to understand what your page is actually about.

None of these require a rebuild. They're usually an afternoon of focused edits — the hard part is knowing to look for them.

Where to start

If you can only fix one thing this week, make it the title tags — they're the fastest to fix and among the highest-impact. From there, work down the list. Or if you'd rather have someone else audit it for you, that's exactly the kind of thing I help with.
