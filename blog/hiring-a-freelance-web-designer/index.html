What to Expect When You Hire a Freelance Web Designer

If you've never worked with a freelance designer before, the process can feel like a bit of a black box. Here's what a reasonably well-run project actually looks like, start to finish.

The timeline

A single-page site typically takes 1-2 weeks; a multi-page business site takes 2-4 weeks. The biggest variable isn't the designer's speed — it's usually how quickly you can provide content (text, images, logo files) and how many rounds of feedback the project goes through.

What you'll need to provide

  • Content — the actual words for each page. A designer can help polish this, but starting from a blank page for your own business copy is slow and expensive.
  • Branding basics — a logo if you have one, and any specific colors or fonts you want to keep. If you don't have these yet, say so up front.
  • Reference sites — two or three sites whose look or feel you like, even loosely. This does more to align expectations than a written brief.
  • Timely feedback — the single biggest thing that speeds up or slows down a project is how quickly you review drafts and respond.

How revisions usually work

Most freelancers include a set number of revision rounds in the price — commonly two. This isn't a trick to charge you more; it's what keeps a fixed-price project from turning into unpaid, unlimited work for the designer. Ask up front what's included and what additional rounds cost, so there's no surprise later.

Questions worth asking before you sign on

  1. What's included in the price, and what isn't? (Stock photos, copywriting, and ongoing maintenance are common add-ons, not always included by default.)
  2. Who owns the final files and the domain/hosting once the project is done?
  3. What happens after launch — is there a support window, or is it fully hands-off?
  4. Can they show examples of past work, even informal ones?
A clear scope in writing, before any design work starts, is the single best predictor of a project going smoothly.

The short version

Good freelance relationships run on clarity: a fixed scope, a realistic timeline, and knowing exactly what's included. Anyone unwilling to put that in writing before starting is worth a second look.
